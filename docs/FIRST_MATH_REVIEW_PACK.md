## Percentages, Profit and Loss

Objectives: calculate percentages, recover an original amount, calculate profit/loss relative to cost price, and calculate discounts relative to marked price.

### Understand
A percentage means out of 100. To find p% of an amount, multiply the amount by p/100.
To express one amount as a percentage of another, divide the first by the reference amount and multiply by 100. The reference amount must be nonzero.

Profit = selling price − cost price (when selling price exceeds cost).
Loss = cost price − selling price (when selling price is below cost).
Percentage profit or loss = profit or loss divided by cost price, multiplied by 100.
Discount is measured against the marked price, not cost price.

### Worked examples
1. A bag of rice costs ₦48,000 and sells for ₦55,200.
   - Profit = 55,200 − 48,000 = ₦7,200.
   - Percentage profit = 7,200 ÷ 48,000 × 100 = 15%.
2. A shop sells a marked item for ₦4,000 after a 20% discount.
   - The buyer pays 80% of the original marked price.
   - Original price = 4,000 ÷ 0.8 = ₦5,000.

### Common mistakes
- Dividing profit by selling price: divide by cost price instead.
- Subtracting 20% of the discounted price to recover the original: divide by 0.8 instead.
- Confusing percentage points with relative percentage change: moving from 20% to 30% is 10 percentage points, or a 50% relative increase.

## Simultaneous Equations

Objectives: solve two linear equations by elimination and substitution, check solutions in both equations, and form equations from a word problem.

### Understand
A simultaneous solution satisfies both equations at the same time. Elimination removes one unknown by adding or subtracting equations after matching coefficients. Substitution replaces one unknown with an equivalent expression from the other equation.

### Worked examples
1. Solve x + y = 5 and x − y = 1.
   - Add equations: 2x = 6.
   - x = 3.
   - Substitute: 3 + y = 5, so y = 2.
   - Check: 3 − 2 = 1.
2. Solve y = 2x + 1 and x + y = 10.
   - Substitute: x + 2x + 1 = 10.
   - 3x = 9, so x = 3.
   - y = 2(3) + 1 = 7.
   - Check: 3 + 7 = 10.

### Common mistakes
- Adding equations without matching coefficients: multiply an entire equation first when needed.
- Changing only one term when multiplying an equation: multiply every term, including the right side.
- Checking only one equation: the solution must satisfy both.
